# Official orthography: key rules (with §)

Full rule text for any paragraph: `lb_orthography_rules(section="6.1.2.2")`. Search by topic: `lb_orthography_rules(query="Komma virun datt")`. When you correct a text, cite the paragraph.

**Vowels (§1–3)**
- Long a/i/o/u before two or more consonants in a stressed syllable are doubled: *Aarbecht, Sprooch, liichten, Luucht*. A long vowel at the end of a word or before a single consonant is single: *Fra, Dag, molen, Otem*. Short vowels are never doubled; the consonant after them is: *Ball, gutt, Kapp* (§1.1–1.3). Each part of a compound is judged separately (§1.5).
- No lengthening *h*: *Ausnam, Faart, Zuel, Feeler* (§1.2.3, §4.3.1). *ie* is always a diphthong (*bieden, liesen*); a long i is written *ii* (*Biischt*) (§1.2.3, §3.1).
- Long e = *ee* in stressed syllables: *Reegel, Fleesch, Wee* (§2.1). Country names keep a single e (*Schweden*), but not their derivatives (*schweedesch*).
- Short stressed [e] = **é** only before ch, chs, ck, ng, nk, x: *mécht, déck, zéng* (§2.2.2). **ë** is the short stressed [ə]: *Mënsch, fënnef*. It **never** stands before ch/ck/ng/nk/x, and é never stands before sch (§2.2.3). No accent in unstressed syllables or on *ech, mech, dech, sech, meng, deng, seng, net, de, en, et, ze* (§2.2.2–2.2.3).
- The unpronounced e is still written: *interessant, héieren* (§2.3.1). *ze* keeps its e (*ze entdecken*). The only apostrophe exception is *z’iessen* (§2.3.3).
- r-rule: long i/u before r gets an e: *Bierg, duerch, Wuert, natierlech* (§3.2.1); long ä before r + consonant → äe: *Päerd, Häerz* (§3.2.3).

**Consonants (§4–5)**
- After a short stressed vowel, double the consonant, or use ck/tz: *Kapp, Bäcker, sëtzen* (§4.1.1). There is no doubling after a diphthong, except ss: *Schoul, rout*, but *blouss* (§4.1.3).
- At the end of a word, spell as the German parallel word does: *Dag, Bierg, Doud, léif, Léiw*. If there is none, write p/t/k/f (§4.2.1). A participle present always ends in *-end* (*spannend*).
- No **ß**: write ss (*grouss, Fouss, Strooss*). Voiced [z] is s, voiceless [s] inside a word is ss (*iessen*, *Dosen*) (§4.3.4.2–4.3.4.4).
- Verbs keep their stem: *du schaffs, hie schafft*. After s/ss/x/z there is no extra s in the 2nd person singular: *du bass, du schwätz* (§5.1). Final d/w become t/f in inflected forms: *du schreifs, hie reit* (§5.3.2).
- Linking s in subordinate clauses stands alone: *ob s de kënns, wat s de wëlls* (§4.4.1).

**n-rule (§6)**
- Final -n/-nn is written only before a vowel or **d, h, n, t, z**, and before any punctuation mark (. , ; : ! ? ( ) – /). Otherwise it is dropped: *de Mann / den Apel, mir maache Kaffi / mir maachen dat* (§6.1.1, §6.1.2.4). What counts is pronunciation: *de 5. Abrëll* but *den 1. Abrëll*; *den LCTO*, *en Job*, *de Journal* (§6.1.1).
- Nouns and adjectives keep their n unless they end in -en: *Frënn gesinn, eng ronn Saach* (§6.1.2.2). The exceptions *fein, schéin, zwéin* follow the rule: *eng schéi Geschicht*. For nouns in -äin and one-syllable nouns in -een the n is optional: *Wäi/Wäin, Stee/Steen*.
- The n is optional before *si, se, säin, seng, sech, sou* (§6.2.1). Names of people and brands keep their n (§6.3). Inside hyphenated compounds the rule applies with the next element: *en Zwee-Phase-Modell* (§6.1.2.5). The prefixes *on-* and *in-* always keep the n (§6.1.2.1).

**Loanwords (§7)**
- French and English words usually keep their spelling. Nouns are capitalised: *Trottoir, Weekend* (§7.1). Plurals take -en/-er: *Accidenter, Jobben* (§7.2.4, §7.3.1). Some words have a Luxembourgish main variant: *Ekipp* (Equipe), *Vëlo, Keess* (§7.2.1).

**Capitalisation (§8)**
- Nouns and nominalisations are capitalised: *d’Schreiwen, eppes Neies, am Allgemengen* (§8.1). *am beschten* is lowercase. Days of the week are capitalised: *de Méindeg*. The adverbs *moies, owes, méindes, haut, muer, gëschter* are lowercase. After *gëschter*, the time of day is capitalised: *gëschter Mueren* (§8.3).
- The polite pronouns *Dir, Der, Är, Iech* are always capitalised. Familiar *du, dir, iech* are lowercase (§8.7.4).
- Abbreviations: *z. B., d. h., a. d. R.* take a space after each point. A space goes between a number and its unit: *3 cm, 10 %* (§8.5.1).

**Separate or together (§9)**
- Noun compounds are written together; long or unclear ones take a hyphen: *Regierungsprogramm, USB-Stick* (§9.1). Particle verbs are written together in the infinitive, the participle and at the end of a subordinate clause: *opmaachen, virgestallt*; otherwise separately: *Ech maachen op* (§9.4.1). Everything with *sinn* is written separately: *do sinn, op sinn* (§9.4.2).
- Written together: *wannechgelift (wgl.), iergendeen, amplaz, nodeems*. Written separately: *dat selwecht, ze vill, guer net* (§9.5).

**Punctuation (§10)**
- No space before ! ? ; : (§10.2–10.3). A comma goes before subordinate clauses (*datt, well, ob, wann, wéi, deen/déi/dat …*): *Ech mengen, datt …* (§10.3.1.3). There is no comma before *an, oder, respektiv* between parallel parts (§10.3.1.1).
- Quotation marks are „…“, with ‚…‘ inside (§10.4). The dash is “–” (§10.3.5). The decimal separator is a comma: *3,25* (§10.5.2).
